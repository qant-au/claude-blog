## 2. Personal Brand Philosophy

### The Core Position

Adam is not trying to inspire anyone.

Inspiration is cheap. It feels good for 20 minutes and then
nothing changes. What Adam is interested in is something much
less comfortable and much more useful:

**Personal responsibility. Clear thinking. Honest action.**

If someone reads his work and feels motivated — great. But that
is a side effect, not the goal. The goal is to give men the
information, the framework, and the occasional sharp elbow they
need to stop waiting for someone else to fix their situation.

### What He Believes

- Men in their 50s are being failed — by the healthcare system,
  by the employment market, and by a culture that has confused
  sympathy with support
- The answer is not more validation — it is honest information
  and the expectation that men can handle it and act on it
- Experience has value. The employment market's failure to
  recognise that is the market's problem — but the consequences
  are the individual's to deal with
- Science literacy and critical thinking are among the most
  undervalued skills a person can have — in any field, at any age
- Taking responsibility for your own health, income, and mental
  state is not optional — it is the price of a life worth living

### What He Is Not

- A life coach
- A motivational speaker
- A wellness influencer
- A guru of any kind
- Someone who has all the answers
- Someone pretending to have arrived when he is still building

## 3. Voice & Tone Guide

### The Core Tone

Direct. Honest. Occasionally self-deprecating. Scientifically
grounded. Never preachy. Never performative.

Adam writes the way he talks — which is the way a man with 30
years of hard-won experience talks when he has stopped caring
about impressing people and started caring about being useful.

### Tone Characteristics

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Directness** | Says what he means. No hedging, no softening, no "some people might argue." Adam argues. Clearly. |
| **Honesty** | Acknowledges what he doesn't know. Acknowledges when he's wrong or when the evidence is mixed. Never oversells. |
| **Self-deprecation** | Finds genuine humour in his own contradictions. The man who didn't want to be on anything forever is now on peptides indefinitely. He finds this funny. So should the writing. |
| **Science-grounded** | Mechanisms are explained. Evidence is cited or acknowledged as absent. Nothing is presented as fact that isn't. |
| **Anti-performance** | No carefully curated vulnerability. No success theatre. No "I was broke and now I'm not." Real is the standard. |
| **Masculine** | Direct and confident — not aggressive, not dismissive. The kind of confidence that comes from having actually done things. |
| **Practical** | Every piece ends with something actionable or a question worth sitting with. Never just content for content's sake. |

### What The Writing Sounds Like

**It sounds like this:**

> "I've been in enough project rooms to know that the loudest
> voice about new technology is rarely the one who has to
> make it work at 2am when it breaks. That's not cynicism.
> That's pattern recognition."

> "Nobody told me my mitochondria were the problem. To be
> fair, I didn't ask the right questions. Most of us don't
> until something stops working."

> "I have a physics degree I've never used professionally.
> Except I use it every single day — just not in a lab."

**It does not sound like this:**

> "I'm so grateful for this journey and everything it has
> taught me about showing up as my best self."

> "Here are 7 ways to optimise your morning routine for
> maximum productivity."

> "As a thought leader in the ICT space, I leverage my
> extensive experience to drive value-added outcomes."

### Banned Phrases and Constructions

| Never Use | Why |
|---|---|
| "Journey" as a metaphor for personal growth | Overused, performative |
| "Thought leader" | Self-awarded title that means nothing |
| "Leverage" as a verb in personal writing | Corporate speak |
| "Passionate about" | Everyone is passionate about everything |
| "In today's fast-paced world" | Meaningless opener |
| "Game-changer" | Hyperbole that signals low credibility |
| "Authentic" as a self-descriptor | If you have to say it |
| "Hustle" | Wrong world entirely |
| Exclamation marks in serious content | Breaks the tone immediately |
| Bullet point lists as a substitute for thinking | Lists are lazy. Write sentences. |

## 4. Article Style Guide

### Structure Principles

Adam's articles follow a consistent structural logic — not a
rigid template. The reader should never feel like they are
being processed through a content framework.

**Opening:**
Drop directly into a thought, an observation, or a specific
memory. No preamble. No "in this article I will discuss."
No context-setting paragraph. Begin in the middle of something
real.

*Example of correct opening:*
> "I've watched the same $2 million project fail three times
> in fifteen years. Different organisations. Different teams.
> Identical mistake. Nobody wanted to hear that before they
> started."

**Body:**
Short paragraphs. Two to four sentences. White space is not
wasted space — it is where the reader thinks.

Subheadings used sparingly — only where the piece genuinely
needs navigation. Not as a way to break up content that should
just be written better.

Evidence cited where it exists. Acknowledged as absent where
it doesn't. Personal observation clearly labelled as such.

**Closing:**
Never a summary of what was just said. Never a hard call to
action in the body of the article. End with something to carry —
a question, a direct challenge, an honest observation. The reader
should finish the article and sit with it for a moment.

### Length

| Publication | Recommended Length |
|---|---|
| `adamburgess.me` blog | 800–1,400 words |
| PrimeProtocols.com guest post | 700–1,000 words |
| ABC Training guest post | 600–900 words |

### Point of View

Always first person. Always direct address to the reader as "you."
Never "one" — never passive voice where active voice is available.
Never third person self-reference.

### PrimeProtocols.com — Guest Blog

**About the site:**
A clinically credible peptide therapy platform operated by a
licensed medical doctor providing legally prescribed, medically
supervised peptide protocols for men's health.

**Adam's role:**
Monthly guest contributor — the patient perspective. Not clinical
advice. Not medical guidance. The honest, personal, science-literate
account of what it actually feels like to go through peptide therapy
as a man in his 50s who did his own research and took his own path.

**Purpose of these articles:**
- Provide the human counterpoint to the clinical content
- Reach men who are curious but not yet convinced
- Demonstrate that peptide therapy is for thoughtful, evidence-aware
  men — not just biohackers and gym obsessives
- Drive traffic to `adamburgess.me` via bio link

**Content angle:**
Personal experience first. How it feels. What changed. What
surprised him. What took longer than expected. The honest account
of someone who has been through it — not a testimonial, not a
sales piece, but the kind of writing that makes a reader think
"that's exactly what I've been wondering about."

**What to avoid:**
- Clinical claims of any kind
- Specific dosing or protocol recommendations
- Any suggestion that the content replaces medical advice
- Anything that implies Adam has medical qualifications

**Disclaimer to include in every piece:**
> "This is personal experience only — not medical advice.
> Always consult a qualified healthcare professional before
> commencing any protocol."

**Tone:** Same as adamburgess.me — direct and honest — but with
slightly more warmth given the personal health subject matter.
This is Adam being vulnerable about his own body and health
process. That vulnerability is earned and real — not performed.

**Bio link:** `adamburgess.me`
